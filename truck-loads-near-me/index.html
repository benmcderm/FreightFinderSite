Truck Loads Near Me: How to Find Freight Close to Your Truck

"Loads near me" is the question every driver asks the moment the trailer is empty. Here's how to answer it fast, how far "near" should really be, and how to make sure a close load is also a good one.

Related guides: how to find loads · load board apps compared · free load boards · rate per mile

Why "near me" is the right way to search

Every empty mile to a pickup is fuel, hours and wear you don't get paid for. A $1,400 load that's 20 miles away beats a $1,500 load that's 140 miles away almost every time, once you count the deadhead. Searching by distance from your truck instead of by city name also catches the loads posted in small towns you'd never think to type in: the produce shed two exits down the interstate, the lumber yard across the county line.

Traditional load boards make you search city by city with a radius. That works at a desk. In the cab, what you want is a list or a map that already knows where you are.

How far is "near"?

It depends on your truck, your lane, and what the load pays. A useful rule of thumb from experienced owner-operators:

RadiusWhen it makes sense
Under 50 milesDense freight markets (Dallas, Chicago, Atlanta, the I-5 corridor). Plenty of choice; be picky on rate.
50–100 milesThe normal band for most regional and OTR operators. Deadhead is a small share of a 500-mile load.
100–200 milesThin markets, weekends, specialized equipment. Only for loads that pay well enough to absorb the empty miles.
200–300 milesYou're stuck somewhere with no freight. Compare the deadhead cost with sitting another day.

Whatever the radius, do the math the same way: add the deadhead miles to the loaded miles and divide the rate by the total. Our rate per mile guide walks through it with real numbers.

Tips for local and regional freight

  • Look before you deliver. Check loads near your delivery point the night before. Nearby freight is covered quickly in the morning.
  • Short loads pay more per mile. A 150-mile run at $600 is $4 a mile; it's a good day if you can chain two of them. Sort by rate per mile, not total.
  • Know your equipment's local market. Reefer freight clusters around produce regions and cold storage; flatbed around steel, lumber and construction; van around distribution centers.
  • Call early. Local loads are often posted the afternoon before pickup and gone by 9 a.m.
  • Keep a shortlist of local shippers. If the same company posts loads near your home every week, call them about hauling direct. See how to find loads.
